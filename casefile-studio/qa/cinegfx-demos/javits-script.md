## Chapter 1 — Javits and the life he was building

Javits Frimpong was 15 and had moved from Ghana to New York to live with his father, Thomas Frimpong, so he could continue his education in the United States, his father recalled. Thomas Frimpong worked as a cab driver. His father remembered the arrival: “I think it was like 4:30, I picked him up from JFK, and in fact, he was very, very, very excited.” He also said Javits “was living the dream.”

At DreamYard Preparatory School, Javits was a tenth-grade student. His father and his sister said he hoped to become a physician. His sister also said he wanted to join the military. “He was so ready that he made me save my uniform for him,” his sister recalled. The Daily News reported that Javits wrote in a diary about hopes for his future.

NYPD Chief of Detectives Joseph Kenny said Javits would leave his phone at home so he could focus on schoolwork and described him as trying to do well.

## Chapter 2 — The walk to school

On Thursday, October 29, Javits was walking to school; Kenny said he was shot as he ran. The reported shooting location was near East 171st Street and Crotona Place, by Crotona Park, in the Bronx. It happened at around 9:30 that morning. He was taken to St. Barnabas Hospital and pronounced dead there that day. ## Chapter 3 — What investigators said

Chief of Detectives Joseph Kenny outlined the police account at a November 2 briefing. Three hooded people wearing surgical masks had been in Crotona Park before Javits arrived, and all three gave chase. The Daily News and CBS New York each reported obtaining surveillance video showing Javits running from a person in dark clothing. Kenny said Javits dropped his backpack, which a passerby took; police said it had not been recovered. Kenny said the three returned to collect shell casings, went through Freeman Street station, jumped a turnstile, boarded a southbound No. 5 train and were tracked into Manhattan.

Kenny said robbery and mistaken identity were possibilities. He said police had found no indication Javits was involved in gang activity. The Daily News reported that Kenny characterized Javits as the embodiment of the American dream. No motive had been publicly announced as of November 13.

## Chapter 4 — Bullying reports and school response

Javits’s father, Thomas Frimpong, said the principal called him in the days before the shooting. His father recalled, “He said two boys were fighting with my son, and the two boys got suspended.” His father believes the shooting may have been retaliation. On November 2, asked whether Javits had been bullied, Kenny called it “one aspect that we’re looking at, as well as some others.” He also said the New York City Department of Education would not provide student information without a subpoena. The department said student records were protected by law and that it would comply with lawful requests. The November 5 vigil came one week after the October 29 shooting; on November 6, the family retained attorney Ben Crump, and the following day’s community walk and candlelight vigil ended near East 171st Street. ## Chapter 6 — arrest and court process

On November 11, police announced that a 17-year-old had been arrested and charged with murder. The 17-year-old faced charges of murder, manslaughter and criminal possession of a weapon, police said. After the arrest, Mayor Mamdani said the city remained committed to justice for Javits and full accountability.

Under New York’s Raise the Age law, most 16- and 17-year-olds charged with felonies begin in Youth Part, with a statutory process that can allow transfer to Family Court. On November 12, the 17-year-old appeared in Youth Part. NBC New York, citing law-enforcement sources, said the 17-year-old did not attend DreamYard. Ben Crump said prosecutors told the family they did not believe those responsible went to Javits’s school or were involved in the earlier incident there. Javits’s father had said he believed the shooting was linked to that incident.

As of November 13, the 17-year-old is being held without bail; police are still seeking two others, and the Bronx district attorney’s office said a grand jury is due to consider the case on November 16. At a press conference on November 11, Javits’s mother said, “I need justice for my child.” Javits’s funeral is scheduled for November 20.
